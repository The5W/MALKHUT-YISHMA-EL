Jewish Records on the Kingdom of Ishmael (MALKHUT YISHMA'EL)

Primary Source Texts • מַלְכוּת יִשְׁמָעֵאל • Medieval History

In classical medieval Jewish literature, Islamic civilization and the Caliphates were specifically designated by the term Malkhut Yishma'el (מַלְכוּת יִשְׁמָעֵאל) — "The Kingdom of Ishmael". Historical accounts, Midrashic texts, and authentic correspondence document the rise of this civilization as a pivotal turning point for the survival of the Jewish nation following centuries of severe oppression under the Roman/Byzantine Empire (Edom).

1. Midrash Nistarot de-Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (7th Century CE) Apocalyptic Manuscript

Composed during the early Islamic expansion across the Near East, this Jewish apocalyptic text records a dialogue between Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and the Angel Metatron regarding the destiny of the Jewish people under Roman rule and the arrival of Islam:

"כיון שראה את מלכות ישמעאל שבאים... אמר: וכי לא די לנו מה שעשתה לנו מלכות אדום הרשעה, שבאים עלינו גם ישמעאלים? מיד ענה מטטרון: אל תירא בן אדם! שאין הקב"ה מביא מלכות ישמעאל אלא כדי להושיעכם מזו הרשעה..."

Scholarly Translation:

"When he saw the Kingdom of Ishmael (Malkhut Yishma'el) coming... he said: 'Is it not enough what the wicked Kingdom of Edom (Rome) has done to us, that we must also endure Ishmael?' Immediately Metatron answered: 'Fear not, son of man! For the Holy One, blessed be He, **brings the Kingdom of Ishmael solely to save you from that wicked power**...'"

2. Liberation of Jerusalem by Caliph Umar (637 CE)

Prior to 637 CE, the Byzantine Empire strictly banned Jewish individuals from entering or residing in Jerusalem for over 500 years (since the Hadrianic expulsion). Authentic documents from the Cairo Genizah (Taylor-Schechter Collection, Cambridge University) record the capitulation of Jerusalem to Caliph Umar bin Khattab:

  • Ended the centuries-old exile banning Jewish people from the holy city of Jerusalem.
  • Permitted 70 Jewish families from Tiberias to return, settle, and establish a designated quarter near the Temple site.
  • Granted the status of Ahl al-Dhimmah, guaranteeing safety of life, property, and the freedom to observe religious law (Halakha).

3. Moses Maimonides (Rambam) on Pure Monotheism (Tawhid)

The foremost medieval Jewish legal authority and philosopher, Moses Maimonides (Rambam), who lived under Islamic rule in Al-Andalus and Cairo, provided formal theological recognition in his magnum opus, Mishneh Torah (Hilkhot Melakhim 11:4):

Mishneh Torah — Rambam

"All these matters of Jesus of Nazareth and of the Ishmaelite (Prophet Muhammad) who arose after him serve only to pave the way for the King Messiah... for now the entire world has been filled with the words of the Scripture and the Laws of God, and knowledge of God's absolute oneness (Tawhid) has spread to distant islands."

4. Rescue of Sephardic Refugees (1492 CE)

When the Catholic Monarchs of Spain issued the Alhambra Decree (1492) expelling all Jewish residents, the Ottoman Empire under Sultan Bayezid II dispatched imperial decrees (*Fermans*) to welcome and shelter Sephardic Jewish refugees.

In the Hebrew historical chronicle Seder Eliyahu Zuta (1523 CE), Rabbi Elijah Capsali recorded the famous declaration attributed to Sultan Bayezid II deriding King Ferdinand of Spain:

"You call Ferdinand a wise king, he who has impoverished his own country and enriched mine with knowledgeable men!"

5. Historical Pattern: When Europe Persecuted, Islamic Civilization Protected

Historical documentation confirms an objective, recurring pattern: whenever Jewish communities faced severe discrimination, mass expulsion, or massacres across Christian Europe, Islamic territories consistently served as a legal sanctuary and safe haven for them.

A. Visigothic Brutality (694 CE) vs. Arrival of Islam (711 CE) 7th Century Contrast

Visigothic Europe (17th Council of Toledo):

King Egica decreed total enslavement for all Jewish citizens, confiscation of property, and mandatory forced baptism of Jewish children.

Islamic Civilization (Tariq bin Ziyad):

Restored full civil rights, emancipated them from slavery, and entrusted Jewish citizens with garrisoning the key cities of Cordoba, Seville, and Toledo.

B. Crusader Massacres (1099 CE) vs. Liberation by Saladin (1187 CE) The Crusades

European Crusader Forces (1099 CE):

Upon capturing Jerusalem, Crusaders burned the main synagogue with the Jewish population trapped inside and massacred Jewish communities along the Rhine.

Sultan Saladin (1187 CE):

Recaptured Jerusalem, defeated the Crusaders, and issued an official public decree inviting exiled Jews back to resettle in the holy city.

C. European Mass Expulsions (1290–1492 CE) vs. Ottoman Millet System Sanctuary System

European Mass Expulsions:

England expelled all Jews (1290 CE), France (1306 CE), Spain (1492 CE), Portugal (1497 CE), accompanied by the Inquisition and public *Auto-da-fé* burnings.

Ottoman Protection:

The Ottoman Sultan deployed his navy to rescue over 150,000 Jewish refugees, granting legal status (*Millet*) and economic freedom in Salonica, Istanbul, and Safed.

D. Nazi Holocaust Genocide (1939–1945 CE) vs. Rescue by Muslim Figures World War II

European Nazi Holocaust:

Nazi Germany's industrial extermination system murdered 6 million European Jews in concentration camps and gas chambers.

Muslim Asylum & Rescue:

The Grand Mosque of Paris (Si Kaddour Benghabrit) forged Muslim identity certificates saving hundreds of Jews. Muslim-majority Albania sheltered 100% of its Jewish population (*Code of Besa*).

Academic Reference: Sir Martin Gilbert, In Ishmael's House: A History of Jews in Muslim Lands (Yale University Press); Heinrich Graetz, History of the Jews (Vols. 3 & 4).

6. Travelogue of Benjamin of Tudela (1165–1173 CE)

In his celebrated work Masa'ot Binyamin (The Itinerary of Benjamin of Tudela), the European Jewish traveler Benjamin recorded the thriving condition of Jewish life in Baghdad under the Abbasid Caliphate. He witnessed the Caliph holding the Jewish Leader (*Resh Galuta / Exilarch*, Daniel ben Hasdai) in high honor and granting dozens of synagogues full freedom to operate in the heart of the empire.

7. Correspondence of Hasdai ibn Shaprut in Cordoba (c. 960 CE)

Hasdai ibn Shaprut, a Jewish scholar and physician who served as vizier to Caliph Abd al-Rahman III in Al-Andalus, recorded in his letter to the Khazar King how Islamic rule provided protection, economic prosperity, and political honor to the Jewish people while their brethren in Europe suffered severe oppression.

8. Golden Era of Jewish Jurisprudence in Safed, Palestine (16th Century CE)

Under the patronage of Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, the town of Safed in Palestine blossomed into the preeminent spiritual and intellectual capital of world Jewry. It was here that Rabbi Yosef Karo authored the Shulchan Aruch—the authoritative code of Halakha followed by Jewish communities worldwide to this day.

9. Historical Records: Prominent Jewish Rabbis Who Embraced Islam Prophetic Era

In the early historical records of Yathrib (Medina), there is undeniable evidence documenting senior Rabbis, Torah scholars, and spiritual leaders who examined the Prophetic credentials from scripture and embraced Islam:

1. Rabbi Abdullah bin Salam (Original name: Al-Husayn bin Salam — Banu Qaynuqa)

Chief religious leader and highest Rabbi of the Jewish community in Medina who accepted Islam after verifying the signs of prophecy.

2. Rabbi Mukhayriq (Senior Scholar & Intellectual — Banu Nadir)

Wealthy scholar and leader who studied Islamic teachings, fought alongside Muslims at the Battle of Uhud, and bequeathed his date groves to the Prophet.

3. Rabbi Zayd bin Su'nah (Foremost Torah Scholar — Banu Qaynuqa)

One of the most revered Torah scholars in Medina who specifically tested the character and forbearance of the Prophet before declaring his faith.

4. Rabbi Thala'bah bin Sa'yah (Spiritual Leader — Banu Qurayza)

A respected spiritual guide and leader of Banu Qurayza who bore witness to the message of Tawhid and accepted Islam.

5. Rabbi Usaid bin Sa'yah (Spiritual Leader — Banu Qurayza)

Prominent religious figure who affirmed his faith after matching Torah prophecies with the qualities of Prophet Muhammad.

6. Rabbi Asad bin Ubaid (Spiritual Leader — Banu Qurayza)

Numbered among the pious scholars and leaders of Medina who wholeheartedly embraced monotheism (Tawhid).

7. Yamin bin 'Umair (Nobleman & Leader — Banu Nadir)

A chieftain and nobleman of the Banu Nadir tribe who chose the path of Islam and supported the Muslim community.

Historical Exegesis & Analytical Note

"Although history records the indisputable fact that their highest rabbis, Torah scholars, and chief religious authorities—such as Rabbi Abdullah bin Salam, Rabbi Mukhayriq, and Rabbi Zayd bin Su'nah—verified the Prophetic signs and accepted Islam, the broader Jewish populace largely chose to remain within their ancestral fold. Truth was established before them through the testimony of their own spiritual guides; however, tribal allegiance, socioeconomic standing, and reluctance to recognize prophecy outside their lineage led the majority to decline the message."

10. Comparative Matrix: Roman/European Law vs. Law of Malkhut Yishma'el

An objective comparative analysis based on medieval legal documents illustrates the fundamental distinction between European-Christian rule and Islamic governance regarding Jewish citizens:

Legal Aspect Byzantine / European Empire (Edom) Malkhut Yishma'el (Islamic Civilization)
Freedom of Worship Banned (*Codex Justinianus*), synagogues confiscated or destroyed. Protected under *Ahl al-Dhimmah*, freedom to maintain houses of worship.
Judicial Autonomy None. Halakhic law deemed illegal heresy under imperial statute. Full autonomy for rabbinical courts (*Beit Din*) over civil and family matters.
Residency in Jerusalem Strict exile & total ban for 500+ years (Hadrian & Heraclius decrees). Restored by Caliph Umar (637 CE) & Sultan Saladin (1187 CE).
Public Office Access Barred from public service, military, or land ownership. Open access: Viziers, Royal Physicians, Finance Ministers (Maimonides, Hasdai).

11. Letter of Rabbi Obadiah of Bartenura from Jerusalem (1488 CE)

Prior to the influx of Spanish refugees, the Italian Rabbi and commentator Obadiah of Bartenura traveled to Jerusalem under Mamluk rule. In an official letter to his father in Italy, he observed the justice of Muslim rule toward the Jewish minority:

"The Muslims in this land are very just and respect the rights of the Jews. Should any Muslim attempt to commit an injustice or seize Jewish property, the Muslim judges (*Qadi*) do not hesitate to penalize the Muslim offender and defend the rights of the Jews."
